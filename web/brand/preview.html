Three marks, each one meaning something

A. The citation bracket A square bracket is the universal mark for a source. Every cited claim ever written ends in one. It is also two posts with a gap, so the same shape is the passage you are trying to get through, and the dot is the claim being held.
B. The moved line Three lines of a rule, and the bottom one has shifted. A diff. It means the thing nobody else is selling: the requirement moved, and we caught the day it moved.
C. The hollow seal The milled edge of an official seal with nothing in the middle. The source is official, and the empty centre says we are not the ones issuing it and will never wear a crest.
